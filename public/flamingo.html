Spot 02 · The latest film

Hold, Please

45.7 seconds · 1920 × 1080 · 24 fps · Mix at -16 LUFS

Frankie has been on hold with “the big guys” so long the timer has stopped being polite about it: 47 minutes, then three hours, a cobweb growing from his foot to the coffee mug. The robot keeps telling him his call is important to them. He has started to doubt it.

So he hangs up and calls flaminGO. One ring, and Maya answers: a real person. “One plan, one price, done.” There is only one problem. After three hours of it, Frankie has grown attached to the hold music, and he would like to be put back on hold, please. By request.

The film was built the way the first spot was built: one master frame of Frankie, approved first, and every other Frankie frame a single-change edit of it, animated in Google Flow with the camera locked off so the hold timer and the phone graphics could be pinned in the edit. Maya’s picture and lip sync were kept from the first version of the spot, the complete version built in Runway; her voice was replaced, at its original timing, the same way Frankie’s was in the first film. The final cut is a hybrid: a Runway performance and a Flow character, finished as one piece.

F1 · The master frame

“Is it, though?”

The frame every other Frankie frame was edited from. Phone on speaker, hold timer running at 47:12, and a stare that has been waiting a very long time.

Robot: “Your call is important to us.” Frankie: “Is it, though?”

F2 · Three hours later

The cobweb

Same frame, one change: Frankie is asleep on one leg and the timer has jumped to 3:12:47. The cobweb from his foot to the mug does the rest of the joke.

Robot, again: “Your call is important to us.”

A real person

Frankie pecks the phone, hangs up, dials flaminGO, and Maya answers on the first ring. One ring, says the counter in the corner. No hold, says the stamp.

F4 · Startled

“Wait… a person?!”

Three hours of robot will do this to a bird. The wobble, the flap for balance, and the double take are all in the frame the clip was animated from.

It is all sorted in one sentence, so Frankie leans in with the only request left. Wing to his chest, hopeful, slightly embarrassed.

Frankie: “Hey, Maya? Could you put me on hold anyway? I kind of miss the music.”

Spot 01 · The first film · The story

Big-Carrier Bill: one long bill, one small flamingo.

34.7 seconds · 1920 × 1080 · 24 fps · Mix at -16 LUFS

Marcus winces at a phone bill so long it spills across the table: “Oof. That’s a big-carrier bill.” Standing on one leg beside his coffee mug is Frankie, a flamingo about the height of the mug, who has opinions about that bill.

Frankie makes the whole pitch from the furniture: balancing on the bill, pointing at the phone, standing on the rim of the mug, riding on Marcus’s shoulder for the switch line. Then the button. Marcus finds Frankie settled into a nest woven from the old bill itself. “Is that… my old bill?” “Finally good for something.”

Packshot, an announcer, and Frankie’s closer: “Switch to pink.” End card, with the sung jingle: “Let’s go, flaminGO!”

Meet Frankie

A mascot is a consistency problem.

Frankie had to be the same bird, at the same size, in a photoreal kitchen and a pink studio, standing next to a mug, a phone and a man, ten shots in a row. The system that held him together was built before a single shot: seven master references, approved in order, and a single rule that the references always win. Shot prompts name what each attached image is and describe only the shot; they never re-describe Frankie in words, because text competes with the picture.

R3The size lock: Frankie a little taller than the mug, a little shorter than the phone, attached to every Frankie shot 3 maxingredients per shot, never more EditSmall faults fixed with an edit prompt, never a regenerate

For “Hold, Please” the same rule got stricter. One master frame of Frankie on hold, approved first, and every other Frankie frame a single-change edit of it: asleep, startled, sheepish. The bird who falls asleep under a cobweb is the bird who wakes up, because both frames are the same frame.

The character lock. Coral pink, not magenta, cream beak with a charcoal tip, one leg tucked.

R2 · Frankie, face

Same face, eye colour and beak as R1, close enough to check the expression work.

R3 · The size reference

Frankie between the mug and the phone. Every shot’s scale comes from this one image.

R4 · Marcus, portrait

Early thirties, mustard sweater over a white tee. Attached to every shot he is in.

R5 · Marcus, full body

Same face as R4, for the wide shots.

R6 · The kitchen

Window on the left in every kitchen shot. No pink in the room; pink arrives with Frankie.

Warm coral backdrop and the white phone, screen a plain blush glow, ready for the logo to be placed in the edit.

Two productions

Same carrier, same bird, two different problems.

The first spot had to make a mascot stay on model for ten shots. The second had to make him funny in one room, at one table, mostly standing still, and it had to fix a first version, built in Runway, that had not worked for Frankie, while keeping the parts of it that had. The system underneath barely changed; what changed is how hard it leans on one frame.

Voice & sound

Keep the performance. Change only the voice.

The animation performances already had the right timing, with beaks and mouths moving on the right syllables. So the dialogue was extracted shot by shot, run through Speechify Studio Voice Changer into one consistent voice per character, and put back at its original timestamps. Timing never changed, so the sync held, measured at 0 ms offset on every line.

Frankie is voiced with Speechify’s Lindsey, accent on; the announcer is Julien. Late in the edit, two of Frankie’s lines were re-recorded as text to speech and fitted word by word to the existing beak movement, landing within 40 ms of the originals. The score is a Suno tropical-pop bed at 104 BPM, cut on bar lines and ducked under every line, with a sung Suno jingle on the end card. Sound design is deliberately small: paper rustles, three soft pops for the signal bars, a ceramic tink as Frankie settles on the mug rim, and a twinkle on the closer. Final mix at -16 LUFS.

“Hold, Please” adds two voices and one instrument. Maya’s performance in the Runway version was already right on camera, so her picture and lip sync were kept and only her voice was replaced, the same extraction and replacement at the original timestamps; the phone robot is flat text to speech, because a robot should sound like one. And the hold music, a full track of cheerful nothing, plays through a phone speaker for the first half of the film and in full for the button, when Frankie sways to it with his eyes closed. You can put yourself on hold with it, further up this page.

Graphics & brand

Built like a carrier, coloured like a flamingo.

The logo is my artwork: the bird on one leg, “flamin” in plum, “GO” in pink, MOBILE in spaced capitals between two pink rules. Captions are white pills with a pink icon disc and plum text, set in Fredoka, with Figtree for labels and fine print. A wordmark bug sits in the bottom right corner through the film and leaves as the end card arrives. Supers never cover Frankie, a face, or the phone screen.
