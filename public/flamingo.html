The story

One long bill, one small flamingo.

Marcus winces at a phone bill so long it spills across the table: “Oof. That’s a big-carrier bill.” Standing on one leg beside his coffee mug is Frankie, a flamingo about the height of the mug, who has opinions about that bill.

Frankie makes the whole pitch from the furniture: balancing on the bill, pointing at the phone, standing on the rim of the mug, riding on Marcus’s shoulder for the switch line. Then the button. Marcus finds Frankie settled into a nest woven from the old bill itself. “Is that… my old bill?” “Finally good for something.”

Packshot, an announcer, and Frankie’s closer: “Switch to pink.” End card, with the sung jingle: “Let’s go, flaminGO!”

Meet Frankie

A mascot is a consistency problem.

Frankie had to be the same bird, at the same size, in a photoreal kitchen and a pink studio, standing next to a mug, a phone and a man, ten shots in a row. The system that held him together was built before any shot was: seven master references, approved in order, and a rule that the references win. Shot prompts name what each attached image is and describe only the shot; they never re-describe Frankie in words, because text competes with the picture.

The film, shot by shot

Ten shots, one joke, told straight.

These are the approved start frames the clips were animated from. Each motion prompt described movement only, then the locks: Frankie keeps his size, shape and colours; Marcus keeps his face; one continuous, unbroken shot, no cuts.

S01 · The big bill

The problem, at full length

The bill unfolds into a concertina strip long enough to spill off the table. No Frankie yet; just the wince.

Marcus (voice): “Oof. That’s a big-carrier bill.”

S02 · Frankie appears

Let the reveal land

No graphics on this shot at all. A small wave, a tilt of the head, and the name tag arrives beside him.

Frankie: “Hi! Frankie. From Flamingo.”

S03 · Double take

Polite disbelief

Shot past Frankie in soft focus, so the size joke reads in a single frame: tiny bird, full-size reaction.

Marcus: “You’re… a flamingo.”

S04 · The pitch

Standing on the evidence

Frankie delivers the coverage line standing on top of the bill itself. The coast-to-coast caption stays clear of him, lower left.

Frankie: “A small one. Big savings, though.”

S05 · Unlimited

A locked-off camera, on purpose

The camera does not move in this shot, so the three signal bars could be pinned to the phone screen in the edit, popping on one by one.

Frankie: “Unlimited talk, text and data.”

S06 · Balance act

The hardest frame in the film

Built by editing the approved S02 frame rather than generating fresh, so Frankie stays identical. Foot on top of the rim, toes curled over it, wings level.

Frankie: “No contracts. No hidden fees. Plans from twenty-five bucks a month.”

S07 · Shoulder ride

The switch, at eye level

Frankie rides the shoulder and nods along with the phone. The phone screen stays turned away from camera.

Frankie: “Switching’s easy. Keep your number. Keep your phone.”

S08 · The nest

The button, built late

This ending was written after the third cut, when the original closer was not landing. The premise pays off: the bill becomes the nest. It replaced the earlier shot outright.

Marcus: “Is that… my old bill?” Frankie: “Finally good for something.”

S09 · Packshot

The carrier moment

Into the pink studio. The stacked logo is tracked onto the phone screen through the push-in, and the price badge sits in the empty left third.

Announcer: “Flamingo Mobile.”

S10 · Switch to pink

Room for the end card

Frankie holds the left third and salutes; the right two-thirds were composed empty so the end card could build beside him.

Frankie: “Switch to pink.”

Voice & sound

Keep the performance. Change only the voice.

The animation performances already had the right timing, with beaks and mouths moving on the right syllables. So the dialogue was extracted shot by shot, run through Speechify Studio Voice Changer into one consistent voice per character, and put back at its original timestamps. Timing never changed, so the sync held, measured at 0 ms offset on every line.

Frankie is Speechify’s Lindsey, with the accent on. The announcer is Julien. Two lines were re-recorded as text to speech late in the edit and time-fitted word by word onto the existing beak movement. The music is a Suno tropical-pop bed at 104 BPM, cut on bar lines and ducked under every line, plus a Suno sung jingle for the end card. Paper rustles, three soft pops for the signal bars, a ceramic tink as Frankie settles on the mug rim, and a twinkle on the wink. Mix at -16 LUFS.

Graphics & brand

Built like a carrier, coloured like a flamingo.

The logo is David’s artwork: the bird on one leg, “flamin” in plum, “GO” in pink, MOBILE in spaced capitals between two pink rules. Captions are white pills with a pink icon disc and plum text, set in Fredoka, with Figtree for labels and fine print. A wordmark bug sits in the bottom right corner through the film and leaves as the end card arrives. Supers never cover Frankie, a face, or the phone screen.

Why a fake carrier gets a real site

The page is part of the proof.
