Production process

A clear, collaborative production process.

Every project follows an agreed creative and production framework, with defined deliverables, timelines and pricing established before production begins.

01

Brief & proposal

We begin by understanding your objectives, audience, creative direction and intended use. You’ll receive a project proposal outlining the recommended approach, scope, timeline and fixed price.

02

Creative development

The creative concept, script and visual direction are developed and presented for approval. Key visual references and hero frames establish the look and feel of the production before moving into animation and video generation.

03

Production

The approved creative direction guides the production of each shot. Visual consistency, performance, composition and continuity are carefully reviewed throughout the process, with progress previews at agreed milestones.

04

Final delivery

The completed production is edited, refined and delivered in the agreed formats and specifications. Two structured rounds of revisions are included.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How long does a production take?

Most individual films are completed within two to three weeks following approval of the creative concept. Timelines vary depending on the scope and complexity of the project. If you are working toward a specific launch or campaign date, we can establish a production schedule around that deadline.

What do you need to get started?

A brief outlining your product or service, target audience, objectives and intended distribution channels is a good starting point. Existing brand guidelines, product photography and other creative assets are helpful, but they aren’t essential. If you’re starting with an idea rather than an established visual identity, creative development can be included in the engagement.

Is the production created using AI?

Yes. AI is an integral part of the production process, allowing for greater flexibility in visual development, environments, casting and cinematography. However, the creative direction, storytelling, shot composition, editing and final execution are carefully directed and reviewed throughout production. The technology supports the creative process; it doesn’t replace it.

Who owns the finished production?

Usage and ownership arrangements are established in the project agreement before production begins. The agreed terms will be outlined in your proposal.

Can you produce Japanese-language versions?

Yes. I offer English and Japanese production capabilities. Language versions can be developed as part of the original production, with appropriate attention to dialogue, voice performance and cultural context.
