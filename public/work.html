Problem one · The idea

You have an idea. You need it finished, and finished well.

Most creative work does not fail at the idea. It fails in the long middle, where the idea gets diluted, delayed or handed to a process that cannot carry it. I direct work end to end, from the first conversation to the finished piece, and you see a near-final version early, so the look is agreed before production, not argued after it.

Concept and script

The idea made specific: what it says, what it shows, why it will land.

Direction end to end

One director across concept, production and finish. Nothing gets lost between departments because there is no handoff to lose it in.

Proof early

A near-final version up front. You approve the real thing, not a description of it, and changes after that stay minor.

AI-native production

Films and campaigns made with AI tools under human direction, at a standard that stands next to conventionally produced work. The six films on this site are the evidence, each with a production guide showing exactly how it was made. Video / Film →

Campaigns that travel

One idea, one visual language, many executions: promotional campaigns, launch materials, sales campaigns and social, all recognizably the same campaign.

Problem two · The brand

It happens quietly: the website says one thing, the sales deck another, the social feed a third. I spent eleven years inside one technology company building the system that stops that drift, and evolving it as the company changed: identity, visual language, websites, and the standards everyone works to.

Brand identity

A visual identity that holds across web, video, social, sales and events.

Visual systems

Colour, typography, composition and motion defined once, applied everywhere.

Websites

Full website rebuilds: structure, content and visual direction.

Content standards

Editorial and visual standards that keep dozens of assets a month on-brand without a review bottleneck.

Consistency that frees people

The system carries the consistency so the team can carry the ideas.

Problem four · The AI question

Your team has the tools. Nobody has made them useful yet.

This is the problem I spend most of my time on now. Licences get bought, a few people experiment, and nothing changes. What changes things is working systems, built inside the real workflow, with a person accountable for the output. That is what I build: production systems colleagues can run themselves, governed so the quality holds. Agents draft, people decide.

A video system people use

An AI video maker I built for colleagues produced 25 branded videos in its first 12 days. It is packaged so non-specialists can make their own, to the house standard.

Knowledge that answers

Company knowledge organized so AI tools draft from approved content, with sources a person can check.

Governance built in

House style, verification flags and approval gates are part of the system, not a policy document nobody reads.

Training that sticks

People learn by making real work with the system, and I teach it the way I learned it: by doing. My Director’s Chair course teaches the same approach. The course →

Problem five · The deadline

Proposals eat weeks, and still read like five people wrote five different ones.

A proposal is an argument with a visual system, produced under deadline pressure by people with other jobs. I direct proposal production the same way I direct anything else: one story, one standard, nothing ships unreviewed, and a drafting system that starts from approved company content so the team spends its time on the argument instead of the assembly.

The system

Requirements → Knowledge → Strategy → Structure → Draft → Evidence → Review → Compliance → Approval.

The result

A complex RFP’s first draft moves from hours of manual work to roughly 30 minutes. Less time assembling information; more time strengthening the argument.
