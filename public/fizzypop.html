01 · What stayed the same

One brief, run twice.

Fizzy Pop came first. Fizola came second, on the same foundations. Holding the world still makes the differences easy to see: anything that changed between the two films was a creative choice, not an accident of the tools.

Maple StreetBrick townhouses in mint, peach and butter yellow, a striped awning, a red hydrant.
JunoThe same face, curls and freckles, restyled for each brand.
Four master referencesThe can, Juno’s portrait, Juno full body and the street, each with one fixed description.
Colour is the storyA faded afternoon, a cold can, and a cola wave that brings the colour back.
The format30 seconds exactly, 1920 × 1080, 24 fps, the drop at about nine seconds.
The pipelineClaude, Google Flow, Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash, Suno and HyperFrames, in that order.

05 · The songs

Two hooks for one summer.

Fizzy Pop!

Jingle · 116 BPM · 68 seconds · Full length below

The first plan was a voiceover over an instrumental. It needed a jingle instead. A 1970s TV jingle crossed with modern funk-pop: bright female lead, gang vocals on the hook, handclaps, slap bass, brass stabs, wah guitar, glockenspiel and a whistle.

Fizzy Pop, Fizzy Pop! Cherry cola, crack the top. Turn it up for the summer crowd. Fizzy Pop… open loud!

The trick is a gap. The intro is sleepy, then one beat of silence. The can crack lands at 9.25 seconds and the drop at 9.61, so the sound lives inside the gap rather than on top of the music.

Download the jingle (MP3)

Fizola

“Turn On The Sunshine” · 112 BPM · 90 seconds

A full original song rather than a jingle. 1970s funk, soul and disco: slap bass, wah guitar, clavinet, horn stabs, cowbell and congas, and disco strings.

Fi-zo-la, Fizola! Ice-cold sunshine cola. Turn it up for the summer crowd. Fizola… turn on the sunshine!

The song starts before it starts. For five seconds you hear the hook muffled and far away, as if it is playing down the block, then the band enters on the wave.

Download the song (MP3)

One idea carried over: the verse never survives a 30-second cut, so its words move into the graphics. What the ear doesn’t get, the eye gets.

06 · The brands

Built to feel remembered.

Both logos were designed as packaging, not technology marks, and neither is derived from a real cola brand. They aim at the same decade from two directions: Fizzy Pop is bright and cheeky, Fizola is warm and a little sun-faded.

Fizzy Pop Cherry Cola. Heavy rounded italic lettering, stacked and tilted upward, with a starburst and three rising bubbles. A sunflower pill reads CHERRY COLA.

08 · The pipeline

The same machine. Two films.

Every stage was directed by a person and executed with AI-assisted tools. The pipeline did not change between the two films. The decisions did.

StageTool
Shot planning, lyrics, prompts, assemblyClaude (Anthropic)
Reference imagery and keyframesGoogle Flow
MotionGemini Omni 1.1 Flash
MusicSuno
Compositing, graphics, mix, renderHyperFrames (open source)

No cameras, no studio, no traditional editing timeline. Each generated clip was trimmed to its strongest seconds, and every flaw was handled the same way in both films. Cut it. Change it. Re-render it.
