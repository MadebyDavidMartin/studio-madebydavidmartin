Hold, Please: Production Guide

How a 45.7-second spec commercial about being on hold was produced first in Runway, then rebuilt and finished with Google Flow, end to end with AI. The final cut is a hybrid of both productions, and this guide documents each one’s work.

Why this spot exists twice

The first version did not work, so it was rebuilt

The first version of “Hold, Please” (v1 to v4) was produced in Runway, on Gen-4.5. It failed on four counts, and they are worth writing down because they decided everything about the rebuild:

  1. Frankie came back off model: thinner, less fluffy, smaller eyes. Next to the first spot he looked like a different bird.
  2. Frankie never spoke, so a narrator had to explain everything. In the first spot, Frankie carries the jokes himself.
  3. There was no joke, only a sequence of events.
  4. Maya’s voice sounded wrong.

The Runway version was not a sketch or a test. It was a complete, finished commercial: every shot produced, Maya cast and performed, the hold music in place, an end card built. It is the reason this film exists in its final shape, and its strongest work is in the final cut: Maya’s performance runs exactly as it came out of Runway, picture and lip sync unchanged, because it was already right, and the hold music, the track the whole joke hangs on, is that production’s track.

What Runway could not do on this film was hold Frankie to the first spot’s character model across a whole spot. That discipline, approved references that the generator is never allowed to outvote, lives in the Flow pipeline that made the first spot. So the rebuild (v2) goes back to that method for Frankie: Google Flow, the first spot’s own references, Frankie talking, one comic premise that keeps getting worse, a button that pays it off, and the same end card and jingle. The final film is a hybrid in the literal sense: a Runway performance and a Flow character, cut together, voiced, scored and finished as one piece. The strongest parts of that production carried straight into the rebuild, on purpose:

KeptWhy
Maya’s two shots, picture and lip syncHer performance was already right. Only her voice was wrong, and a voice can be replaced without touching the picture.
The hold musicIt was the joke’s raw material in both versions.

The narrator lines from v1 were dropped. The first spot has no narrator, and neither does this one. Frankie tells the story himself.

The story

Three hours on hold, one ring to fix it

Frankie has been on hold with “the big guys” so long it has become absurd: asleep on one leg, a cobweb growing from his foot to the coffee mug. He finally hangs up, calls flaminGO, and a real person answers straight away and sorts everything in one sentence. The twist: after all that, he misses the hold music.

The master frame

Make F1 first; everything else is built from it

Frankie’s frames were made in Google Flow with Nano Banana Pro at 2K, 16:9, attaching the first spot’s own references: R3, the size reference, and R6, the kitchen, with R1, the full-body character lock, as the identity check. The rule from the first spot applies unchanged: the references always win, and the prompt never re-describes Frankie in words.

The F1 prompt, in full: “Use the attached images as exact references and do not redesign anything in them: the size reference photo is Frankie at his exact size (use it for his look and size only) and the room photo is the kitchen. Close shot at tabletop height on the white oak table, the window on the left. Frankie, exactly as in the size reference photo, same head, face and proportions, stands on one leg in the centre of the frame. On his left a modern smartphone in a matte charcoal case lies face up on the table, its screen showing only a plain, soft white glow. A plain white coffee mug stands on his right. He stares flatly straight into the camera with heavy, half-closed eyelids and a deadpan, unimpressed expression, wings folded, like someone who has been waiting a very long time. Locked-off camera with clear empty space in the upper left of the frame. Frankie is tiny, the size of a small toy figurine: from his feet to the top of his head he is no taller than the coffee mug. Style: a modern national TV commercial. The room and any person in it are photorealistic live action, like real film footage, never animated. Frankie is the animated character from the attached reference, unchanged in look, composited in with matching light and a soft contact shadow under his foot. Exactly one Frankie, with two wings and two legs. Cinema camera, 35mm lens, bright natural morning daylight from the window on the left, gentle shallow depth of field, clean modern colour grade. Full-frame 16:9 image. No text, no logos, no watermarks.”

Three checks before anything was built on it: Frankie matches R3 (fluffy, big round head, big brown eyes, not the thin first-version Frankie); the phone screen is plain, with no icons or text; and the size sits where the first spot put him against the mug. One production note on that last check: the prompt asks for a bird no taller than the mug, and the frame that passed sits a little taller, where R3 itself puts him. Consistency with the reference won over the prompt’s wording, which is the system working as designed.

F2, F4 and F5 are edits, not new frames

Each remaining Frankie frame was made by attaching F1 alone and changing one thing:

  • F2, asleep: eyes closed, head drooped low, body slumped, and a thin cobweb from his foot to the handle of the mug.
  • F4, startled: leaning toward the phone, eyes wide, beak open, both wings lifting.
  • F5, sheepish: leaning down close to the phone, hopeful little smile, one wing tip pressed shyly to his chest.

Two repair prompts cover the failures this method produces, and nothing else was needed:

  • Too big: “Edit this image. Only change: make the flamingo one third smaller, keeping his foot on exactly the same spot, his pose and his look unchanged, and fill the space he no longer covers with the matching background. Nothing else changes.”
  • Off model (frame plus R3 attached): “Edit this image. Only change: make the flamingo match the attached reference exactly, with the same head size, eyes, beak, feathers and proportions, at the same small size, pose and position. Nothing else changes.”

Animation

Five clips, one locked-off camera

The four Frankie frames, with F1 used twice, were animated in Google Flow with Omni Flash 1.1, frames to video. Every motion prompt describes movement and dialogue only, and ends with the same locks: Frankie keeps his exact size, shape, feather colours and proportions, two wings and two legs; the phone keeps its shape and its plain white screen glow; one continuous, unbroken shot, no cuts.

The camera never moves in any clip, and that is a graphics decision, not a style one. The hold timer, the “Calling flaminGO” screen graphic and the tap marks all had to be pinned to fixed positions in the edit. A moving camera would have made every overlay a tracking job.

Clip checks, applied to every take: the same Frankie in all five clips, at the same size next to the mug; the beak moves on the words and the line is word-perfect; no extra wings or legs; no text appearing on the phone. Maximum three tries per clip before the prompt, not the dice, gets rewritten.

Voices

Keep the performance, change only the voice

Same pipeline as the first spot. The dialogue performances in the generated clips already had the right timing, so the lines were extracted and run through Speechify Studio Voice Changer, then put back at their original timestamps, so the lip sync never moved.

  • Frankie: Lindsey (F), Accent on, the same voice as the first spot. Continuity across spots is a casting decision.
  • Maya: her two shots were kept from v1, picture and lip sync untouched, and only the audio was replaced: two or three warm, natural female voices were tried and the one that sounded like a real support agent, not a presenter, was kept.
  • The robot: Speechify text to speech, a flat automated phone-system voice at normal speed, the line recorded three times and the phone treatment added in the edit. A robot should sound like one.
  • The announcer: Julien, “Flamingo Mobile.”, the same recording family as the first spot’s end card.

Music and sound

The hold music is a cast member

Nothing new was composed for this spot. The Suno tropical-pop bed and the sung “Let’s go, flaminGO!” jingle are the first spot’s, reused so the two films sound like one campaign. The bed starts exactly on Maya’s pickup, drops out under Frankie’s request, and stays out.

The hold music is the exception, and it is treated as a sound-design element with two states: through a phone speaker, tinny and small, for the first three beats and under the sway; and in full for the button’s first moments, when Frankie closes his eyes. It is a complete track of 108 seconds, far more than the film uses, and the full track is playable on the flaminGO page itself, behind a button labelled “Put me on hold”, which seemed only fair.

Graphics and edit

A timer is the whole first act

The overlay package extends the first spot’s system (white pills, plum text, Fredoka, nothing ever covering Frankie, a face, or the phone screen) with a call-centre set: the hold timer pill in the upper left, starting at 47:12 and counting; the jump cut to 3:12:47 that tells three hours in one number; music notes drifting from the phone; the robot’s line as a caption; “tap!” marks as Frankie pecks the screen; “Calling flaminGO” pinned to the phone; a connected pill that reads ON HOLD 0:00 and RING 1; Maya’s name tag; the NO HOLD stamp; the three supers and the $25 price badge; an “A real person?!” stamp; and for the button, the pill that makes the joke official: ON HOLD 0:00, BY REQUEST.

The end card adds the campaign line “Skip the hold.” under the logo build, with Frankie still swaying at the side of the frame, and holds the fine print for the full card, about seven seconds, well past the 1.6-second minimum the production set for itself.

Final checks, as run on the delivered cut

  • Frankie is the first spot’s Frankie in every shot.
  • Each joke lands in order: “Is it, though?”, the timer jump and the cobweb, “a person?!”, “miss the music”.
  • Runtime 45.7 seconds. The plan targeted about 30; the by-request sway and the end card earned the extra length in the cut, and it stayed.
  • Mix at -16 LUFS integrated (measured -16.06), true peak -2.85 dB.
  • No em dashes, no real carrier names, fine print legible at full screen.
