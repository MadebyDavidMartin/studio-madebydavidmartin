Big-Carrier Bill: Production Guide

How a 34.7-second spec commercial for a carrier that does not exist was conceived, built, voiced, scored and finished, end to end with AI.

Concept, direction and production by David Martin, made with AI. Logo supplied by David Martin.

Production steps

The pipeline, in order

  1. References first. Seven master references (R1 to R7) are generated and approved before any shot exists: Frankie full body, Frankie face, Frankie at exact scale, Marcus portrait, Marcus full body, the kitchen, the studio and phone plate.
  2. Start frames. Every shot begins as a still made with Nano Banana Pro in Google Flow, from at most three attached references. Prompts name the references and describe only the shot; the characters are never re-described in words.
  3. Motion. Each approved still is animated with Omni Flash 1.1, frames to video, one continuous shot with no cuts. The model performs the dialogue so beaks and mouths move on the right syllables.
  4. Voice. The generated performances are extracted line by line, converted to the cast voices in Speechify Studio Voice Changer, and replaced at exactly the original timestamps, so lip sync never moves.
  5. Graphics. Captions, the price badge, the corner bug, the phone-screen logo and the end card are built to the brand sheet and composited over the picture.
  6. Music and sound. A Suno tropical-pop bed is cut on bar lines and ducked under dialogue; a Suno sung jingle closes the spot; small synthesized effects mark the jokes.
  7. Edit. Shots are assembled at broadcast timing, mixed to -16 LUFS, and finished as a 1080p master and a faststart web encode.
  8. Checks. Automated checks run over every prompt before generation (238 checks on this production), and the finished film is checked frame by frame against the script.

Story

One long bill, one small flamingo

Marcus winces at a phone bill so long it spills onto the floor: "Oof. That's a big-carrier bill." Frankie, a phone-sized flamingo, is standing on one leg beside his coffee mug: "Hi! Frankie. From Flamingo." Marcus: "You're... a flamingo."

Frankie pitches the carrier while balancing on the bill, beside the phone, and on the rim of the mug: coast-to-coast coverage, unlimited talk, text and data, no contracts, no hidden fees, plans from $25 a month, keep your number and phone. He rides Marcus's shoulder for the switch line.

The button pays off the opening premise: Frankie has turned the old bill into a nest and settled into it. Marcus: "Is that... my old bill?" Frankie: "Finally good for something." Announcer: "Flamingo Mobile." Packshot, then Frankie: "Switch to pink." End card, with the sung jingle: "Let's go, flaminGO!"

The structure is the classic mascot spot: one game (a very small salesman on the furniture), heightening (each pitch point gets a worse perch), then a button that ties back to the first shot, then the call to action.

Look

A CG mascot in a photoreal world

The format is the one insurance and carrier mascots use on television: a Pixar-quality 3D character, about 15 cm tall, photographed as if he were really standing in a real kitchen. Frankie's feathers are warm coral, the colour of an actual flamingo, deliberately kept away from magenta, which is a protected trade colour of a real carrier. No real carrier is ever named; comparison stays with "the big guys", and every claim is covered by the spec-ad fine print.

Scale is the whole illusion, so it is locked by reference R3, Frankie at his exact size between a coffee mug and a phone. R3 is attached to every shot Frankie appears in.

Cast and places

Two performers, two rooms

Frankie

A Pixar-style 3D flamingo, about the height of a coffee mug, warm coral feathers, long bent bill with a dark tip. Cheerful, quick, kind; a friendly neighbour with a great tip, never a shouty salesman. Played by the references R1, R2 and R3 in every appearance.

Marcus

A man in his early thirties in a mustard sweater, warm and mildly baffled, the audience's stand-in. Played by references R4 (portrait, attached to every Marcus shot) and R5 (full body).

The kitchen and the studio

R6 is the photoreal kitchen plate the story plays in; R7 is the pink studio plate for the packshot and the end card, matching the key visual.

The working rules, carried over from Occupied: references win over words. A shot prompt opens by naming what each attached image is, then describes only the shot. At most three ingredients per shot. Every image prompt ends with its full style suffix; every motion prompt ends with its locks, an Audio line, and "One continuous, unbroken shot, no cuts." A nearly right still is fixed with a single-change edit prompt ("Edit this image. Only change: ... Nothing else changes."), never regenerated from scratch.

Shots

Production notes

  • S01 to S07 were generated as planned: start frame from the shot's references, one continuous motion clip of 6 to 8 s, dialogue performed by the model for lip sync.
  • S08 became S08b. The original closer had Marcus stand up tall while Frankie delivered the line "Now you're standing tall." Across three cuts it never landed: the rise read as slow motion and the line tied itself to a wide shot. The replacement, proposed on comedy structure (one game, heightening, a button that pays off the premise), is the nest: the long bill from S01 becomes Frankie's bed. The start frame was built by the edit route for identity, from the approved S06 frame with R3 and R4 attached. New dialogue was recorded by the same extract, convert, replace route as every other line, and Marcus's laugh and Frankie's contented sigh from the generated clip were kept in the mix.
  • S09 carries the logo on the phone screen, tracked to the push-in. In v5 the price badge was timed to leave a quarter second earlier so it is fully gone one frame before the cut to S10.
  • S10 was framed with Frankie already in the left third, so the end card panel needs no reframe when it slides in.

Voice plan

Keep the performance, change only the voice

Flow's generated dialogue is already in sync with every beak and mouth movement, so the timing is treated as untouchable. Each character's lines are extracted into one file with a timing map; the file is run through Speechify Studio Voice Changer with a single chosen voice; every line is then placed back at its original timestamp. Measured on this production, converted lines landed at 0 ms offset against the originals, with envelope correlation of 0.87 to 0.96.

  • Frankie: Speechify Voice Changer, voice Lindsey (F), Accent on. Brief: a cheerful, quick-witted voice in its thirties, warm and slightly raspy, neutral North American, bright comic timing, upbeat but never shouty, no announcer read.
  • Marcus: Speechify Voice Changer on his generated lines. Brief: relaxed, warm, mid-low, natural and conversational, mildly baffled, good-humoured, not a performer. For v5, David recorded the opening line himself in Speechify, level-matched to Frankie, and S01 was lengthened slightly so the "Hi!" still has its gap.
  • Announcer: Speechify text to speech, Julien (M), speed and pitch at zero. Brief: a warm, clear, confident mid-range Canadian read in his early forties, polished television commercial delivery for a trusted local service, friendly and reassuring, never salesy.
  • Fitted lines: two Frankie lines were re-recorded as text to speech and fitted word by word to the existing beak movement with pitch-kept time stretching, landing within 40 ms of the original onsets.

Score and sound

Suno bed, Suno jingle, small sounds

The bed. Suno tropical pop, C major, 104 BPM: ukulele strums, marimba, light steel drum accents, finger snaps and handclaps, warm round bass, a cheeky whistled hook, sunny and confident rather than silly, instrumental. The bed is cut on bar lines, joined into its own breakdown under the final third so the last hit lands exactly on the end card, and ducked about 9 dB under every line of dialogue.

The jingle. A short Suno sung jingle, "Let's go, flaminGO!" The second sung phrase is used, from its pickup beat, placed so "Let's go" and "flaminGO!" land with the on-screen words as the end card builds.

Effects. Small and dry, mostly synthesized: paper rustle and slide under the bill, a soft pop on Frankie's reveal, a tiny wing flutter on the wave, a foot tap on paper, three soft pops as the signal bars appear, a ceramic tink as Frankie settles on the mug rim, a shimmer on the packshot, and a twinkle on the closer. Marcus's laugh and the paper sounds inside the nest shot are the generated clip's own audio, kept.

Edit

Five cuts, v1 to v5

  • v1 (30.0 s). First full assembly: all ten shots, overlays, music join, mix at -16 LUFS. The mechanics worked; the brand was replaced the same day.
  • v2 (33.6 s). The flaminGO rebrand: overlays recoloured to pink and plum, name tag rewritten, the stacked logo on the packshot phone, the corner bug added, the end card rebuilt on blush with the sung jingle. The S08 stand-up was evened out to the standard 1.115x after a faster section read as unnatural. Runtime was allowed to grow past 30 s rather than squeeze the jokes.
  • v3 (32.4 s). The name tag moved right so it never covers Frankie's face; the S08 rise retimed; Marcus's lines raised to Frankie's level; the end card build tightened to the sung words.
  • v4 (34.1 s). S08 replaced outright by S08b, the nest, with new voices by the standard route. Corner badge simplified to the wordmark alone, larger.
  • v5 (34.7 s), final. The opening line re-recorded by David in Marcus's voice and placed at the head; S01 lengthened by half a second and everything after moved 13 frames later; the packshot price badge leaves earlier so it never overlaps Frankie across the cut.

Prompt integrity

Checked before anything was generated

238 automated checks passed on the prompt set, including: every image prompt ends with its full style suffix and carries exactly one; every shot prompt opens by naming its attached references, uses at most three ingredients, and contains no competing word-description of Frankie, Marcus or the kitchen; every Frankie shot has a size anchor; every Marcus shot attaches R4; every motion prompt ends with locks, an Audio line and the no-cuts line; on-camera lines in motion prompts match the script word for word; no real carrier names, no magenta, no borrowed mascot traits.

Finishing

Specs and deliverables

  • Master: 1920×1080, 24 fps, H.264 with stereo AAC, -16 LUFS integrated, true peak -3 dB, about 29 MB.
  • Web: the same cut, faststart H.264, about 10 MB, the copy that plays on the studio site.
  • Stills: frames from the final cut at the name tag (4.2 s), the captions (16.9 s), the nest (23.0 s and 26.5 s), the packshot with price badge (28.4 s) and the end card (33.8 s).
  • Also kept: all ten clean start frames plus the nest frame, the key visual in 16:9 and 4:5, references R1 to R7, the logo set (original, stacked, wordmark, bird, corner badge), per-shot dialogue stems, the music bed and jingle, and every earlier cut.
