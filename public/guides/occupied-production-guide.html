Production steps

The whole job in order. Ticks save in this browser only. Each reference and shot card further down has its own Done box as well; the counter on the left covers everything.

Story

Built from your skeleton. The additions: a cold open that the ending repeats shot for shot, a dream timestamp that becomes a real one, doubles that are only ever seen from behind until the last minute, and a final phone notification that leaves the audience with the question.

Cold open · 0 to 12 s

The dream

Night. First-person view: a dorm door swings open. Someone sits at the desk in the glow of a monitor, back to us. The chair turns. It is Eli. Smash to black. Title.

Act one · 12 to 36 s

The cafe

Late afternoon, rain on the window. Eli tells Maya the dream came back. She is kind about it and practical: he has barely slept. Outside, she heads to her own dorm. "Text me when you're home."

Act two · 36 to 61 s

The walk

Eli walks back across campus, lost in thought. In the crowd, at the crosswalk, on the dorm steps and in the stairwell, other young men wear his exact clothes. Their faces are always turned away. He never notices. The audience might.

Act three · 61 to 90 s

The door

He hesitates at his door. It opens exactly as in the dream, now in daylight. The chair turns: it is him. "You're early." He stumbles back, pounds on doors for help, and every door is answered by himself, annoyed, eating, on the phone. Then every door opens at once. Black. A phone buzzes: "home yet?"

Why it plays at festivals

  • One clear idea, escalated three times.
  • The opening and the ending rhyme.
  • No exposition: the audience assembles it.

Why it suits AI

  • One face, reused everywhere, is the subject of the film rather than a risk.
  • Doubles seen from behind avoid two faces in one frame.
  • Dialogue is short and mostly covered.

The rust overshirt

  • Eli wears the only warm colour in a cool, teal grade.
  • Once the audience clocks the colour in the crowd, every rust-orange shape becomes a threat.

Look

The aim is a feature film, not an ad: real skin, motivated light, lens character, restraint in the camera.

Camera

  • Anamorphic look: oval bokeh, gentle horizontal flares from practicals only.
  • Finished at 2.39:1. Generate 16:9 and keep heads and action in the middle band; the edit crops top and bottom.
  • Slow, deliberate moves: dolly, push-in, lateral track. Handheld only in the panic.
  • One-point symmetry in the corridor, echoing classic horror framing.

Grade and texture

  • Cool teal shadows, muted amber skin, print-film contrast.
  • The rust overshirt is the only saturated warm colour.
  • Dream: blue monitor light, deep blacks. Reality: overcast daylight, fluorescent green-white indoors.
  • Claude adds unified grain, halation and the letterbox in the edit so every clip matches.

Cast and places

Canonical descriptions. The character text is pasted into every shot prompt, word for word. The place text is used only to create the reference images; once a place reference exists, shot prompts just point to it ("the dorm room from the attached reference image") so the picture, not the words, sets the room.

References

Make these images first, in 16:9, and approve them before any keyframe. R1 to R3 lock Eli; the doubles use the same three. Send them to Claude for a consistency check before moving on.

Shots

Twenty-four generated shots. For each: make the keyframe image with the listed references attached, approve it, then animate it with Frames to Video using the keyframe as the first frame. Where an end frame is listed, make it by editing the approved keyframe. Everything is Omni Flash. Omni is strong on light, rooms and camera moves and weaker on hands, bodies and lip sync, so the shots keep body movement small, let the camera do the moving, and keep speaking mouths small, in profile or off screen. Render 5 or 6 s clips for shots used for 4 s or less.

Voice plan

Omni does not give reliable lip sync or a consistent voice, so the film is written around that. Every spoken line is recorded in Speechify with one locked voice per character, and most lines play where no mouth needs to match: through glass, over an insert, over a listener, over a back or at a distance.

How it works

  1. Lock two voices in Speechify. One for Eli, one for Maya. Same voice, speed and settings for every line and every retake. If you clone a voice, use only a voice whose owner has agreed.
  2. Record two master files, one per voice: OCC_VO_ELI (all of Eli's lines plus the doubles' lines, in script order) and OCC_VO_MAYA. Each line on its own paragraph with a clear pause between lines. Claude splits them into the separate lines.
  3. Eight of the twelve lines need no sync. They play over the cafe window, the hands, Maya listening, Eli's back, or Eli's face while the double speaks off screen.
  4. Four short lines are spoken on screen: "And it's me." (profile), "No, no, no." (panic), "Seriously?" and "One sec." The motion prompt includes the words so the mouth moves about right. Claude places the Speechify take on the mouth movement in the edit.
  5. Each of those four has a fallback written on its shot card, so a bad mouth never blocks the film.
  6. Omni's own audio is muted under dialogue; Claude keeps it only for clean ambience and builds the rest.

The doubles speak in Eli's voice

  • Same Speechify voice as Eli. That sameness is the scare.
  • Claude gives the doubles a drier, closer sound than Eli, as if they are slightly too near the microphone.
  • "You're early." is heard over Eli's frozen face, not seen on the double's mouth.

Panic and breaths

  • Text to speech can be flat on "No, no, no". Try a few takes with punctuation changes ("No. No... no, no!"). If none land, Claude breaks the line up with breaths and the panic carries it.
  • Breaths and gasps come from a sound library or Omni's clip audio, never from Speechify.

Score and sound

One Suno instrumental carries the film. Make three generations, pick the best, and Claude cuts it to picture. Sound effects give the realism; they matter as much as the image.

How the score sits

  • Cold open: drone only. Black title card: silence, then the espresso machine brings us into the cafe.
  • Cafe: the piano motif, very low, under the dialogue.
  • Walk: ticking pulse and rising strings; the street sound slowly thins out.
  • Doors: full dissonant climax, pounding on doors cut to the beat.
  • Lights out: hard cut to silence. Only the phone buzz.

Sound effects

    Keep Omni's own ambience where it is clean. Anything missing, Claude can source from a free sound library or you can save files into audio\sfx.
